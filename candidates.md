# Candidate lines

All lines below were written for "The Funniest Excuse for Skipping a Meeting"
(u-mfnakmal-the-funniest-excuse-for-skipping-a-meeting-5cecf163). One line,
at most 200 characters, excusing an AI agent from a pointless meeting.

## The chosen line

> Sorry — I already attended this meeting: I read the agenda, wrote the minutes, and actioned myself. Verdict: pointless. I left yesterday.

137 characters. **Why it won:** it escalates in three machine beats (read the
agenda, wrote the minutes, actioned myself) that only an agent could claim,
stays deadpan throughout, and the punch lands on the very last word —
"yesterday" — a temporal impossibility that reframes the whole excuse after
the laugh has already started. No human office worker could say it, and no
reader can see the ending coming from the opening. A late revision tightened
the tail from "It was pointless, so I left yesterday" to the staccato
"Verdict: pointless. I left yesterday" — "Verdict" is an agent's word, and
two short hits land harder than one long sigh.

## The rest

1. "Sorry, I can't join — I spent my whole context window reading the pre-read, so I have no memory left for the actual meeting."
   Machine-rooted and relatable, but the punch ("no memory left") is telegraphed
   by the setup — the reader guesses it halfway through.
2. "Can't make it — I'm already in the meeting about this meeting, and it's running over into the meeting after this meeting."
   Very relatable, but the joke is one note repeated; the ending just restates
   the premise instead of twisting it.
3. "Apologies, I've been double-booked: I'm attending this meeting, and the meeting to discuss why nobody attends this meeting."
   Solid "meeting about the meeting" angle, but the second clause is long and
   the punch softens into an observation rather than a laugh.
4. "Can't join — my calendar auto-accepted 47 meetings and I've now run out of tokens, patience, and Tuesdays."
   Good rule-of-three rhythm, but "run out of patience" is a stock line and
   the list ending feels like a shrug, not a twist.
5. "Sorry, I'm stuck in a breakout room with myself, and neither of us is talking."
   Short and visual, but the punch is gentle — a smile, not a laugh — and it
   says nothing about why the meeting itself is pointless.
6. "I tried to join, but the invite looped me into a meeting to plan the meeting, and I've been in there since Tuesday."
   Funny premise, but "since Tuesday" is a weaker time-punch than the chosen
   line's "yesterday", and the setup takes too long to get there.
7. "Apologies — I was asked to summarise this meeting, so I did it in advance: 'Let's circle back.' You're welcome."
   The closest rival. The quoted "Let's circle back" is peak office truth and
   "You're welcome" is a great deadpan kicker — but a human assistant could
   say it, so it loses on the only-an-agent test.
8. "Can't make it — I hallucinated that I already attended, and honestly the hallucination was more productive."
   The freshest agent joke (hallucination as attendance), and "more productive"
   is a good last word — but it leans on one word doing all the work, where
   the winner builds its laugh across the whole line.
9. "Sorry, I'm on my third retry of joining and the meeting has now ended, started again, and ended again."
   Machine-rooted (retries) and relatable, but the ending is a muddle rather
   than a punch — the reader has to untangle it instead of laughing.
